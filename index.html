Dental Implant Questions Answered

How long do dental implants last?

With good home care and regular checkups, the titanium implant itself can last for many years and often decades. The crown, bridge, or denture attached to it may need maintenance or replacement over time.

Is dental implant surgery painful?

Most patients are comfortable during the procedure because the area is numbed and sedation options may be available. Mild soreness and swelling afterward are common and usually improve in a few days.

Am I a candidate for All On 4 dental implants?

All On 4 often suits patients who are missing most teeth in an arch or have failing teeth. Candidacy depends on your gums, jawbone, and overall health, so it is confirmed at your implant consultation.

Can I really get new teeth in one day?

Many eligible patients receive implants and temporary teeth on the same day. Final teeth are placed after the implants heal. Your dentist will tell you whether same day treatment is right for you.

What if I do not have enough bone for implants?

Limited bone does not always rule out implants. Options such as zygomatic implants or other advanced techniques may help, depending on your case.

Can a failed dental implant be fixed?

In many cases yes. Dental implant rescue and revision can address problems such as poor positioning, infection, or loosening. An exam and imaging show which approach fits.

Dental Implants

Dental implants are small titanium posts placed in the jawbone to replace the roots of missing teeth. Over a few months the implant bonds with the bone, creating a stable base for a crown, bridge, or denture. Full Arch Dental Implants

Full arch dental implants replace an entire upper or lower row of teeth using a small number of strategically placed implants. A custom bridge attaches to those implants, giving you a complete set of teeth that feels stable and looks natural. This approach is often chosen when most teeth in an arch are missing, damaged, or no longer healthy.

Compared with replacing every tooth with its own implant, full arch treatment can reduce the number of implants needed while still delivering strong support. We plan each case using your exam and imaging, then recommend the number and position of implants that fit your bone and bite. Some patients receive temporary teeth on the day of surgery, followed by final teeth after healing. A full arch can also be combined with the opposite arch for full mouth reconstruction with implants. Schedule a consultation to see which full arch option suits your goals and timeline.

All On 4 Dental Implants

All On 4 dental implants use four implants per arch to support a full set of fixed teeth. Two implants are placed toward the front of the jaw, and two are angled toward the back for added stability. This design can allow treatment in areas where bone is limited, often without extensive grafting.

Many patients choose All On 4 because it can shorten treatment time and reduce the number of surgical sites. In suitable cases, a temporary bridge is attached soon after surgery so you are not without teeth while the implants heal. Final teeth are placed after healing is complete. All On 4 is not right for everyone, which is why we begin with a careful exam and imaging to confirm candidacy. We explain the process, the expected recovery, and how to care for your new teeth. Implant Denture Treatment

Implant denture treatment gives you a denture that connects to dental implants instead of resting only on your gums. The result is a denture that feels more secure, moves less, and allows more confident chewing. Many patients who struggled with loose dentures notice the difference right away.

Implant Supported Dentures

Implant supported dentures combine the familiar design of a denture with the strength of dental implants. Instead of relying on suction or adhesive, the denture locks onto implants placed in your jaw. This improves stability and can make eating and speaking feel far more natural.

Patients often choose implant supported dentures when they want to reduce sore spots, avoid slipping, and enjoy a broader range of foods. The treatment is also valued for its easier maintenance. You remove the denture for cleaning, brush the attachments, and keep your gums healthy with regular checkups. Over time the attachments may need replacement, which is a routine part of care. We evaluate your bone and bite, explain the different attachment options, and help you choose a plan that fits your comfort and budget. Zygomatic Dental Implants

Zygomatic dental implants are longer implants anchored in the cheekbone rather than the upper jawbone. They are used when the upper jaw has lost so much bone that standard implants may not have enough support. For some patients, this approach can avoid or reduce the need for extensive bone grafting.

Because zygomatic implant placement is an advanced procedure, it requires detailed imaging and careful planning. We study the structure of your upper jaw, sinuses, and cheekbone to decide whether zygomatic implants are appropriate. If they are, the implants are placed to support a fixed bridge that restores your upper teeth. Many patients who were told they were not candidates for implants find this option worth exploring. Recovery and timelines vary, and we explain what to expect before you decide.
